Best Golf Course Communities in Scottsdale for Luxury Living

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published August 22, 2026

What makes Scottsdale one of the premier golf destinations for luxury buyers?

Scottsdale has more golf courses per capita than almost anywhere in the country, and a meaningful share of them anchor genuinely luxury residential communities rather than simply sitting adjacent to housing. The desert terrain itself is part of the appeal — Scottsdale's courses are known for dramatic elevation changes, natural desert landscaping between fairways, and mountain backdrops that make even a mid-round photo look like a postcard. For buyers, that combination of course quality and setting is a major driver of value in the communities built around them.

Which North Scottsdale communities stand out for golf-focused buyers?

Silverleaf and Desert Mountain sit at the top of the market, both guard-gated with multiple award-winning courses, resort-caliber clubhouses, and some of the highest price points in the Valley — Silverleaf's median list price has run in the $6M+ range. Troon and Troon North offer a similar caliber of golf with a broader range of price points, while DC Ranch and Whisper Rock round out North Scottsdale's premier golf communities with their own distinct architectural styles and club cultures.

What about golf communities closer to central Scottsdale?

McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch combine golf with Scottsdale's lake system, giving residents both course and waterfront living in a more central location than the North Scottsdale communities — a real advantage for buyers who want quick access to Old Town, Fashion Square, and Sky Harbor without sacrificing the golf lifestyle. These communities also tend to offer more architectural variety, from 1980s ranch-style homes to fully remodeled contemporary properties.

Do you have to be a golfer to enjoy living in these communities?

Not at all. Many buyers in Scottsdale's golf communities are drawn by the lifestyle infrastructure that comes with them — clubhouses, fitness centers, tennis and pickleball, fine dining, and a built-in social calendar — more than the golf itself. The wide, quiet streets, mature landscaping, and consistent home quality that golf communities tend to maintain are also a draw for buyers who simply want a well-kept neighborhood, whether or not they ever play a round.

What should golf-community buyers ask before purchasing?

Club membership structure varies significantly between communities — some bundle a membership into the HOA, others require a separate initiation fee and dues that can run well into five figures annually, and a few are semi-private with no membership requirement at all. It's also worth asking directly whether a home's lot backs to a fairway, tee box, or green, since each has different implications for privacy, noise, and the risk of stray golf balls. I walk every golf-community buyer through these specifics before they fall in love with a house, so there are no surprises after closing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

What is the most exclusive golf community in Scottsdale?

Silverleaf and Desert Mountain are generally considered Scottsdale's most exclusive golf communities, both guard-gated with award-winning courses and some of the highest price points in the Valley.

Do I have to join the golf club if I buy in a golf community?

It depends on the community. Some bundle club membership into the HOA, others require a separate initiation fee and annual dues, and a few Scottsdale golf communities are semi-private with no membership requirement at all.

What's the difference between North Scottsdale and central golf communities like McCormick Ranch?

North Scottsdale communities like Silverleaf and Desert Mountain offer higher-end course quality and guard-gated privacy, while central communities like McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch combine golf with lake access and a more central location near Old Town.

Is it more expensive to live on a golf course in Scottsdale?

Generally yes, though the premium varies by lot position. Homes backing to a green or scenic fairway typically command a higher price than interior lots within the same community.

Can non-golfers enjoy living in a Scottsdale golf community?

Yes. Many residents value the clubhouse amenities, well-maintained streets and landscaping, and built-in social calendar these communities offer independent of whether they play golf.
