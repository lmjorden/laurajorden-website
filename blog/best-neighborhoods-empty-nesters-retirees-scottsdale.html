Best Scottsdale Neighborhoods for Empty Nesters and Retirees Downsizing to Luxury

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published September 12, 2026

What are empty nesters and retirees typically looking for when downsizing?

The buyers I work with in this stage of life are rarely looking to compromise on quality — they're looking to trade square footage and maintenance for location, ease, and lifestyle. That usually means single-level living, a smaller but more thoughtfully finished home, and proximity to the things they actually use day to day: walkable restaurants, a favorite fitness studio, healthcare, and often an airport that makes visiting grandchildren painless.

Which neighborhoods suit single-level, low-maintenance luxury living?

Gainey Ranch and parts of McCormick Ranch offer excellent single-level villa and patio-home product, often within gated sub-communities with HOA-managed landscaping that genuinely reduces the maintenance burden. Biltmore-area condos and luxury patio homes offer a similar lock-and-leave lifestyle with the added benefit of proximity to both Phoenix and Scottsdale. For buyers who still want a single-family home but on a smaller, more manageable lot than they had while raising a family, parts of North Scottsdale's established (non-guard-gated) communities offer that middle ground well.

What about buyers who want walkability and amenities over acreage?

Old Town Scottsdale has become an increasingly popular choice for downsizing buyers who want to walk to dinner, galleries, and the farmers market rather than drive everywhere. The trade-off is generally home size and privacy in exchange for a genuinely walkable, socially active daily life — a trade a growing number of downsizing buyers are happy to make once their kids are grown.

What healthcare and lifestyle factors matter most at this stage?

Proximity to quality healthcare is consistently one of the top considerations for retiring buyers, and Scottsdale's Mayo Clinic campus and HonorHealth network give the area a real advantage here compared to many other Sun Belt retirement destinations. Beyond healthcare, buyers at this stage often prioritize an active social community — many of Scottsdale's golf and country clubs have a genuinely vibrant year-round membership base of retirees and semi-retirees, which makes building a new social circle easier than it might be in a purely residential neighborhood.

What should downsizing sellers know about timing their own sale?

Most downsizing buyers are also sellers, and coordinating the sale of a family home with the purchase of a smaller property takes real planning — particularly around whether to sell first and rent temporarily, or buy first with a contingency or bridge financing. I walk every downsizing client through this trade-off early, since the right answer depends heavily on your specific financial picture and how attached you are to moving only once.

Common Questions

What Scottsdale neighborhoods are best for downsizing to single-level living?

Gainey Ranch and parts of McCormick Ranch offer excellent single-level villa and patio-home product, often in gated sub-communities with HOA-managed landscaping that reduces maintenance.

Is Old Town Scottsdale a good fit for retirees?

Yes, for retirees who prioritize walkability and an active social calendar over home size and privacy. Old Town has become increasingly popular with downsizing buyers who want to walk to dinner and galleries.

How is Scottsdale's healthcare access for retirees?

Strong. The Mayo Clinic's Scottsdale campus and the HonorHealth network give the area a real advantage over many other Sun Belt retirement destinations.

Should I sell my current home before buying a smaller one?

It depends on your financial picture. Some downsizing sellers sell first and rent temporarily, while others buy first using a contingency or bridge financing; the right approach should be planned early with your agent and lender.

Are there active adult or country club communities in Scottsdale for retirees?

Yes. Many of Scottsdale's golf and country clubs have a vibrant year-round membership base of retirees and semi-retirees, making it easier to build a new social circle after downsizing.
