Buying Near the Phoenix Mountain Preserve in 85028: What to Check Before You Buy

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published November 1, 2026

What should you check before buying a home near the Phoenix Mountain Preserve in 85028?

Before buying near the Phoenix Mountain Preserve in 85028, check four things: whether the open land behind the lot is actually preserve, whether the lot falls under Phoenix hillside rules, how close the nearest trailhead sits, and what comparable preserve-edge homes have sold for. Each one affects daily life and resale value.

The Shea-Tatum corridor is within the City of Phoenix and borders the Town of Paradise Valley, so Phoenix rules apply rather than Scottsdale or Paradise Valley rules. Laura Jorden walks buyers through each item before an offer, because the answers change street by street, and sometimes lot by lot, along the preserve edge. For a broader overview, see the Shea-Tatum corridor area guide and Moving to the Shea-Tatum Corridor.

Can the desert behind a preserve-edge lot ever be developed?

Land inside a City of Phoenix mountain preserve is strongly protected. Since 1986, the Phoenix City Charter has required a public vote before any preserve land can be sold, traded, redesignated, leased, or otherwise removed from preserve status. That protection is a large part of why a true preserve-edge lot tends to keep its open view.

The important word is true. Not every stretch of open desert near the preserve is preserve land; some parcels are privately owned or held for other uses and could change. Before counting on a view, confirm who owns the parcel behind the home on the Maricopa County Assessor's parcel map, or ask Laura to check it for you. Source: Phoenix City Charter, Chapter XXVI, Section 5.

How do Phoenix hillside rules affect remodeling a home near the preserve?

If a property has a slope of 10 percent or more, the City of Phoenix treats it as a hillside development area. Building there goes through hillside review, which looks at building height over natural grade, lot coverage, the disturbed area outside the roof footprint, and a required grading and drainage plan.

For buyers planning an addition, a casita, a new pool, or a rebuild, these rules can shape what is possible and how long approvals take. It is worth asking an architect or the city about a specific lot during the inspection period, not after closing. Source: City of Phoenix hillside review page.

How do trailheads affect daily life on nearby streets?

Living near a trailhead puts the trails a short walk away, and it can also mean more parked cars and foot traffic on nearby streets, especially on cool-season weekend mornings. Homes a few streets back often have the same easy preserve access with less activity. The right balance depends on how often you will use the trails.

Trailheads serving the corridor include Dreamy Draw Recreation Area, the 40th Street Trailhead, and Quartz Ridge Trail. Visit a home you are considering on a Saturday morning during hiking season and again on a weekday evening, and note street parking, noise, and lighting before you write an offer.

What preserve rules should dog owners and hikers know?

The City of Phoenix does not allow dogs on its trails when the temperature is above 100 degrees, and leashes must be six feet or shorter year-round. Owners must carry out waste and stay on designated trails. Extreme heat warnings can also bring trail restrictions, so summer access looks different from winter access.

One timely note for 85028 buyers: the city plans to close North Mountain Park for renovations from December 2026 through early 2028, while other Phoenix Mountain Preserve trailheads, including the North Mountain Visitor Center, remain open. Source: City of Phoenix Parks and Recreation.

How should you judge the value of a preserve-edge home?

Judge a preserve-edge home against other preserve-edge sales, not the ZIP code average. Backing directly to protected desert, the width of the view, the slope of the lot, and distance from a trailhead can all move price. Comparing like with like helps buyers avoid overpaying and helps sellers avoid leaving money on the table.

Common Questions

Is the Shea-Tatum corridor in Phoenix or Paradise Valley?

The Shea-Tatum corridor is in the City of Phoenix, in the 85028 ZIP code, along the eastern edge of the Phoenix Mountain Preserve. It borders the Town of Paradise Valley, so buyers often compare the two, but Phoenix city rules apply to homes in 85028.

Can land in the Phoenix Mountain Preserve be developed?

Not without voters. Since 1986, the Phoenix City Charter has required a public vote before preserve land can be sold, traded, redesignated, leased, or removed from preserve status. Buyers should still confirm that the open land behind a specific home is actually preserve land.

What counts as a hillside lot in Phoenix?

The City of Phoenix treats property with a slope of 10 percent or more as a hillside development area. Building or remodeling there requires hillside review of building height, lot coverage, disturbed area, and grading and drainage.

Are dogs allowed on Phoenix Mountain Preserve trails?

Yes, on a leash six feet or shorter, with waste carried out. Dogs are not allowed on City of Phoenix trails when the temperature is above 100 degrees.

Do homes that back to the Phoenix Mountain Preserve sell for more?

Often, but the premium varies with the view, slope, privacy, and distance from a trailhead, so there is no single number. The reliable way to measure it is to compare recent MLS sales of preserve-edge homes with similar homes nearby; Laura Jorden can prepare that comparison for a specific street.
