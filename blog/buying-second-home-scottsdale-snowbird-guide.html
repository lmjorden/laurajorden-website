Buying a Second Home in Scottsdale: A Snowbird's Guide to Seasonal Luxury Living

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published October 17, 2026

Why do so many snowbirds choose Scottsdale for a winter home?

According to the Arizona Office of Tourism, the state welcomes more than 300,000 seasonal residents every year, and Scottsdale is one of the biggest draws among them. The combination of warm winter climate, golf, hiking, dining, and a favorable tax environment compared to many cold-weather and coastal states makes it a natural fit for buyers who want to escape winter without giving up an upscale lifestyle.

When is the best time to buy, and does snowbird season affect pricing?

Seasonal residents typically arrive from late October through April, which is also when buyer demand — and competition for well-located homes in resort-amenity communities — tends to be highest. Buyers willing to shop during the summer off-season often find less competition and more room to negotiate, even though touring a home in 100-degree heat isn't the most flattering first impression of your future winter retreat.

How does Arizona property tax work for a second home versus a primary residence?

Arizona classifies owner-occupied primary residences as Legal Class 3 and second homes, vacation homes, and other non-owner-occupied residential property as Legal Class 4. Class 4 properties don't receive the same homeowner tax relief as a primary residence, so it's worth budgeting for that difference upfront. Statewide, the average effective property tax rate runs around 0.85% of assessed value, and annual increases in assessed value are capped at 5%, which keeps long-term tax growth predictable even without the primary-residence discount.

What should out-of-state buyers know about taxes when they eventually sell?

If you live out of state at the time of sale, Arizona may require withholding a portion of the sale proceeds for state income tax purposes. This is a real consideration to plan around, not a reason to avoid buying — but it's the kind of detail I make sure every out-of-state client understands well before closing, and I'd always recommend confirming current requirements with a CPA or tax advisor as part of your purchase planning.

Which price points and communities perform best for seasonal buyers?

Luxury and mid-tier properties in the $500,000–$1.5 million range perform strongest for seasonal buyers, particularly in Scottsdale communities with resort-style amenities that make sense for part-time living. The broader market has stayed active too — Scottsdale logged 660 single-family closings in August 2026, up from 620 the year before. Both golf and gated communities and low-maintenance luxury condos are popular with snowbirds, since a lock-and-leave property that doesn't demand upkeep while you're away is often just as important as the view.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

How many months a year do most snowbirds spend in Scottsdale?

Most seasonal residents arrive between late October and April, though the exact length of stay varies widely by buyer — some spend the full winter, others a few weeks at a time throughout the season.

Will I pay a higher property tax rate on a Scottsdale second home?

Second homes are classified as Legal Class 4 in Arizona and don't receive the same tax relief as an owner-occupied primary residence (Class 3), so the effective rate is typically higher than what a full-time resident pays on their main home.

Is it better to buy a snowbird home during peak season or off-season?

Buying during the summer off-season often means less competition and more negotiating room, while peak season (late October through April) gives you the chance to experience the community at the time of year you'll actually be living there.

Do I need to withhold taxes when I eventually sell my Arizona second home?

If you're an out-of-state resident at the time of sale, Arizona may require withholding a portion of the proceeds for state income tax purposes. Confirm current requirements with a CPA or tax advisor as part of your planning.

What price range is most common for snowbird buyers in Scottsdale?

Properties in the $500,000 to $1.5 million range in resort-amenity communities tend to perform strongest with seasonal buyers, spanning both golf-community homes and low-maintenance luxury condos.
