New Construction vs. Resale Luxury Homes in Scottsdale: Which Is Right for You?

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published September 5, 2026

What's currently available in new construction across Scottsdale?

New construction in Scottsdale is concentrated mainly in North Scottsdale's master-planned and guard-gated communities, where remaining land allows for it, along with a smaller number of custom infill lots in established neighborhoods like Arcadia and the Cactus Corridor. True new construction in Old Town Scottsdale and Paradise Valley is comparatively rare, since both are largely built out, which means most "new" inventory there consists of recently completed spec homes or ground-up custom builds on a purchased teardown lot.

What are the advantages of buying new construction?

New construction gives buyers modern floor plans, current design finishes, and builder warranties on major systems — real advantages for buyers who don't want to think about a roof or HVAC system for the first decade of ownership. Energy efficiency is typically far better than in homes built even 10 to 15 years ago, which matters in Arizona's climate. Buyers who purchase during the build process may also have the opportunity to select finishes, though that window is narrower than it once was as builders increasingly sell completed spec homes rather than pre-sale.

What are the advantages of buying resale?

Resale homes offer something new construction generally can't: established neighborhoods with mature landscaping, proven lot positioning (established golf-course and mountain-view lots rarely come up as new construction), and often larger lot sizes than current zoning and land costs allow builders to offer today. Resale also tends to be less expensive per square foot than comparable new construction, and in desirable, largely built-out areas like Old Town Scottsdale and Paradise Valley, resale may be the only realistic path to that specific location.

How do costs compare between new construction and resale?

New construction typically carries a premium per square foot over comparable resale, reflecting both current material and labor costs and the value of not needing near-term updates. That said, resale buyers should budget realistically for updates — a home that hasn't been renovated in 15-20 years often needs kitchen, bathroom, and system updates that can close much of the price gap once factored in. The right comparison isn't just purchase price, but total cost including likely near-term renovation.

Which buyers tend to prefer each option?

Buyers relocating from out of state who want a turnkey home with minimal post-purchase work often gravitate toward new construction, especially in North Scottsdale's master-planned communities. Buyers with a strong preference for an established location — Old Town's walkability, Paradise Valley's mature estate lots, Arcadia's classic ranch-home character — are usually better served by resale, since that inventory simply doesn't exist as new construction in those areas.
