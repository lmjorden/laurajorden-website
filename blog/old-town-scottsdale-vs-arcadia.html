Old Town Scottsdale vs. Arcadia: Which Lifestyle Fits You?

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published June 10, 2026

What is the main difference between Old Town Scottsdale and Arcadia?

Old Town Scottsdale is a walkable, urban entertainment district built around condos and infill construction, while Arcadia is a low-density, tree-lined residential neighborhood built around single-family ranch and custom homes. The two are about a 10-minute drive apart, but the day-to-day feel is very different.

How do home prices compare between Old Town Scottsdale and Arcadia?

Entry point$350,000 (condo)$900,000 (ranch home)
Typical single-family$800,000–$1.8M+$1.3M–$2.5M
Luxury / estate tier$2M+ (new construction)$5M–$10M+ (premier estate)
Recent median~$560,000~$1.3M

Old Town Scottsdale has a lower entry price because of its large condo inventory, while Arcadia's entry price is higher because nearly all of its inventory is single-family homes on quarter-acre-plus lots.

How does the housing stock differ?

Old Town Scottsdale is dominated by condominiums, townhomes, and new luxury infill, with limited single-family inventory. Arcadia is dominated by ranch homes, mid-century architecture, farmhouse renovations, and custom estates on quarter- to half-acre lots, with very few condos.

Buyers who want a low-maintenance, lock-and-leave property with minimal yard upkeep will generally find more options in Old Town Scottsdale. Buyers who want yard space, mature trees, and a single-family footprint will generally find more options in Arcadia.

How does the daily lifestyle compare?

Old Town Scottsdale's lifestyle centers on walkability to restaurants, art galleries, and nightlife, with Scottsdale Fashion Square and the Scottsdale Waterfront anchoring the district. Arcadia's lifestyle centers on the Arizona Canal path and neighborhood streets, with well-known restaurants such as La Grande Orange, Postino Arcadia, and Chelsea's Kitchen woven into the residential grid rather than concentrated in a commercial core.

Both neighborhoods offer quick access to Camelback Mountain trailheads, though Arcadia sits directly at its base while Old Town Scottsdale is a short drive away.

How do commute times compare?

Arcadia is closer to Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport and Downtown Phoenix, at roughly 10 to 15 minutes, while Old Town Scottsdale is roughly 15 to 20 minutes from the airport. Both neighborhoods are within an 8- to 10-minute drive of each other, so buyers cross-shopping the two areas can typically tour both in a single afternoon.

Which neighborhood has a stronger rental or investment case?

Old Town Scottsdale generally has a stronger case for income property, since condo and patio-home inventory supports both long-term rental and, in buildings where it's permitted, short-term rental strategies tied to spring training and seasonal visitor demand.

Arcadia's single-family inventory is more commonly purchased as a primary residence or renovation project rather than a rental, given the neighborhood's higher entry price and lower proportion of attached, lock-and-leave properties.

Which neighborhood has better long-term resale appeal?

Both neighborhoods have strong long-term resale reputations within the Valley, but for different reasons. Old Town Scottsdale's appeal is tied to its entertainment district and walkability, which tends to hold value well for condos and newer luxury infill. Arcadia's appeal is tied to its mature tree canopy, canal access, and larger lots, which are difficult to replicate in newer developments and tend to support strong resale for well-maintained single-family homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Is Old Town Scottsdale or Arcadia more expensive?

It depends on the price point. Old Town Scottsdale has a lower entry price due to its condo inventory, starting around $350,000, while Arcadia's entry price starts higher, around $900,000, because its inventory is almost entirely single-family homes.

Which neighborhood is more walkable, Old Town Scottsdale or Arcadia?

Old Town Scottsdale is more walkable to restaurants, galleries, and shopping, since it was built as a dense commercial and entertainment district. Arcadia is walkable and bikeable along the Arizona Canal path and neighborhood streets, but its dining and retail are spread throughout the residential grid rather than concentrated in one walkable core.

Does Arcadia or Old Town Scottsdale have larger lots?

Arcadia has significantly larger lots, typically a quarter- to half-acre with premier properties approaching a full acre. Old Town Scottsdale's inventory is dominated by condos and townhomes with little to no private lot.

Which is closer to Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport?

Arcadia is closer to Sky Harbor, at roughly 10 minutes, compared with roughly 15 to 20 minutes from Old Town Scottsdale.

Can I find single-family homes in Old Town Scottsdale?

Yes, though inventory is limited. Single-family homes in Old Town Scottsdale typically range from $800,000 to $1.8 million or more, compared with a much larger single-family supply in Arcadia.
