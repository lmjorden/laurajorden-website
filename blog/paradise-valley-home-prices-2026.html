Inside Paradise Valley: Resorts, Restaurants, and the Lifestyle Behind Arizona's Most Exclusive Address

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published June 1, 2026

What makes Paradise Valley feel different from everywhere else in the Valley?

Paradise Valley is a separate incorporated town wedged between Scottsdale and Phoenix, and it has spent decades protecting a specific way of life: large estate lots, mountain views, and almost no commercial development. There are no apartment buildings and very few storefronts, so the town reads as quiet and residential even though it sits minutes from some of Arizona's busiest retail and dining districts.

That restraint is what gives Paradise Valley its resort-town feel — the town itself stays quiet, while the world-class resorts, spas, and restaurants within its borders do the entertaining.

What resorts and spas are based in Paradise Valley?

Paradise Valley is home to five of Arizona's most recognized resorts: The Phoenician, Sanctuary Camelback Mountain Resort & Spa, Mountain Shadows Resort Scottsdale, Omni Scottsdale Resort & Spa at Montelucia, and the JW Marriott Scottsdale Camelback Inn Resort & Spa. Each has its own spa, and together they give residents access to world-class treatments, pools, and dining without leaving town.

Sanctuary's spa is built into the side of Camelback Mountain with treatment rooms that open to the desert, while Montelucia's Moroccan-inspired Joya Spa and the Camelback Inn's longtime spa both draw visitors from well outside Arizona. [VERIFY current spa names and offerings before publishing]

Where do Paradise Valley residents eat?

LON's at the Hermosa Inn, set in the former home and art studio of cowboy artist Lon Megargee, is one of the town's signature restaurants, known for a wood-grilled menu and a patio with sweeping Camelback Mountain views. [VERIFY current menu and hours]

El Chorro, a Paradise Valley dining institution known for its sticky buns and mountain views, has served the town for decades, and the resorts add their own destination restaurants on top of that — from poolside dining to fine dining rooms overlooking Camelback and Mummy Mountain.

What outdoor lifestyle does Paradise Valley offer?

Camelback Mountain and Mummy Mountain frame the town on nearly every side, and several trailheads — including the popular Echo Canyon approach to Camelback — sit within or immediately next to Paradise Valley neighborhoods. It's common for residents to start a morning with a mountain hike and be home in time to work from a home office with a mountain view.

Cosanti, architect Paolo Soleri's bronze foundry and studio, remains open to visitors and is one of the town's more distinctive cultural landmarks, alongside the Barry Goldwater Memorial.

What does day-to-day life look like in Paradise Valley?

Because so few neighborhoods are gated, privacy in Paradise Valley comes from lot size and setbacks rather than a guardhouse — most homes sit on one to five acres, with mature landscaping and long private driveways doing the work that a perimeter wall does elsewhere. Wide, quiet roads and minimal through-traffic are part of the same design.

Despite that quiet, quick residential character, Paradise Valley is genuinely central: Old Town Scottsdale, Scottsdale Fashion Square, the Biltmore area, and Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport are all a short drive away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

What resorts are located in Paradise Valley?

Paradise Valley is home to The Phoenician, Sanctuary Camelback Mountain Resort & Spa, Mountain Shadows Resort Scottsdale, Omni Scottsdale Resort & Spa at Montelucia, and the JW Marriott Scottsdale Camelback Inn Resort & Spa, each with its own spa and restaurants.

What is LON's at the Hermosa Inn?

LON's is a celebrated restaurant set in the former home and studio of cowboy artist Lon Megargee, known for wood-grilled, globally inspired fare and a patio with Camelback Mountain views.

Can you hike from a Paradise Valley neighborhood?

Yes. Several trailheads, including popular approaches to Camelback Mountain, sit within or immediately adjacent to Paradise Valley, making it possible for many residents to reach a trail without driving.

Is Paradise Valley a gated community?

No. Paradise Valley is a separate incorporated town, not a single gated community, and most of its neighborhoods are not gated. Privacy comes primarily from large lot sizes, strict zoning, and setback requirements, though a few specific subdivisions are guard-gated.

How central is Paradise Valley to the rest of the Valley?

Very. Despite its quiet, low-density character, Paradise Valley sits minutes from Old Town Scottsdale, Scottsdale Fashion Square, the Biltmore area, and Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport.
