Paradise Valley & Scottsdale Luxury Market Update: November 2026

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published November 9, 2026

What is the Paradise Valley luxury market doing in November 2026?

Paradise Valley homes priced at $1.5 million and above closed at a median sale price of $5,300,000 in October, with 17 closed sales and 196 active listings on the market. Sellers received 95.4% of list price on average, and homes sold in an average of 105 days. Inventory is deep: at October's pace, Paradise Valley's active listings above $1.5 million would take about 11.5 months to sell, which gives buyers real room to negotiate.

Laura Jorden pulls these figures from ARMLS each month so buyers and sellers can see the market for themselves rather than rely on headlines. Paradise Valley is a small town with relatively few sales in any one month, so a single estate sale can move the median noticeably. Read the numbers together rather than any one alone.

What is the Scottsdale luxury market doing for homes over $1.5 million?

Scottsdale homes priced at $1.5 million and above closed at a median sale price of $2,205,000 in October, across 137 closed sales. There are 834 active listings in that range, homes averaged 97 days on market, and sellers received 96.2% of list price. Scottsdale's luxury market is closer to balanced, with about 6 months of supply and 157 homes above $1.5 million under contract.

How can you tell whether it is a buyer's or a seller's market right now?

Divide active listings by one month of closed sales. The result is months of supply: roughly how long current inventory would last at today's pace. A lower number generally favors sellers; a higher number gives buyers more choice and more room to negotiate. This November, Paradise Valley luxury shows about 11.5 months of supply and Scottsdale shows about 6.1 months of supply.

How long are luxury homes taking to sell this fall?

Paradise Valley luxury homes averaged 105 days on market in October, and Scottsdale homes over $1.5 million averaged 97 days. Days on market tells sellers how much patience to plan for, and it tells buyers whether a home that has been listed for a while may have room to negotiate.

Are luxury sellers getting their asking price?

Paradise Valley luxury sellers received an average of 95.4% of list price, and Scottsdale sellers above $1.5 million received 96.2%. Sale-to-list ratio is one of the clearest signals of pricing power, and it is the number Laura Jorden watches most closely when helping a seller decide where to set a list price.

Are Paradise Valley and Scottsdale home values actually rising?

Not really. Across Paradise Valley and Scottsdale combined, October's median sales price was 9.9% higher than a year ago, but that is mostly because a more expensive mix of homes sold, not because the same homes are worth more. Fewer homes under $1 million closed, more $5 million-plus estates closed, and new construction made up twice the share of sales dollars.

Here is a simple example. If one $10 million estate and nine $1 million homes sell one October, and two estates and eight $1 million homes sell the next, the average sale price jumps about 47%, yet no home is worth a dollar more. To see real appreciation, compare similar homes: resale homes in the same price band sold for about the same price per square foot as a year ago, down 1.2% under $1 million, up 1.0% from $1 million to $2 million, and down 4.5% from $2 million to $5 million. Laura Jorden reads a rising average this winter as more expensive homes selling, not as existing homes gaining value.

What do the November numbers mean for luxury buyers?

Look at months of supply and days on market together. Where inventory is deeper and homes are taking longer to sell, there is usually more room to negotiate on price, repairs, or closing timeline. Where homes are moving quickly near full price, preparation matters more: have financing or proof of funds ready before you tour.

Fall is when many seasonal residents return to the Valley, and plenty of luxury buyers tour while they are here. A short list of neighborhoods and a clear budget will make each visit count. What $2M+ Buys You in Scottsdale is a good neighborhood-by-neighborhood starting point.

What do the November numbers mean for luxury sellers?

Price to the market the numbers describe, not the one you remember. A home priced in line with recent closed sales tends to draw its strongest interest in the first few weeks on the market; an overpriced home can sit, invite lower offers, and leave money on the table. Preparation, photography, and timing still matter at every price point.

If you are thinking about listing in the coming months, Selling Your Scottsdale Home for Top Dollar: A Luxury Seller's Timeline walks through each step.
