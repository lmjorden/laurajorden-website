Hiking the Phoenix Mountain Preserve from 85028: A Trailhead Guide for Shea-Tatum Homeowners

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published November 2, 2026

Which Phoenix Mountain Preserve trailheads are closest to the Shea-Tatum corridor?

The 40th Street Trailhead, at 9200 N. 40th Street, sits in 85028 and is the closest city trailhead for many Shea-Tatum homeowners. The 32nd Street Trailhead and the Dreamy Draw Recreation Area, at 2421 E. Northern Avenue, are a short drive south and open up the Piestewa Peak side of the preserve.

Laura Jorden, a Scottsdale luxury real estate advisor with Compass and a Valley resident for more than 35 years, put together this guide for buyers and homeowners in 85028. If you are shopping in the area, walking a trail near the homes you like before you tour is a useful first step. The City of Phoenix lists six trailheads for the Piestewa Peak and Dreamy Draw area, and its trail map shows how the numbered trails link together.

Source: City of Phoenix, Piestewa Peak trail descriptions and trailheads.

What is a good first hike from the 40th Street Trailhead?

The L.V. Yates Trail (#8) is the city's listed trail from the 40th Street Trailhead: a moderate 2.5-mile out-and-back with about 220 feet of elevation gain. It makes a comfortable after-work or weekend walk, and it intersects the Charles M. Christiansen Memorial Trail (#100) for anyone who wants a longer outing.

Because the climb is gentle, it is a good way to get to know the preserve with houseguests, children, or a dog on a cool morning. From the junction with Trail #100, longer routes are possible; check the city's trail map before you head out so you know how far you plan to go.

Source: City of Phoenix, Piestewa Peak trail descriptions.

Which nearby trails offer a bigger climb?

For more elevation, the Quartz Ridge Trail from the 32nd Street Trailhead is a moderate 1.7-mile out-and-back with about 500 feet of gain. The Piestewa Peak Summit Trail is the serious one: the city rates it extremely difficult, with roughly 1,200 feet of climbing over 1.2 miles out and back.

Piestewa Peak rises to 2,608 feet, and the view from the top rewards the effort. The City of Phoenix reports that more than 200 hikers are rescued from city parks and preserves each year, so start early, carry plenty of water, and turn around if the heat or the terrain feels like too much. The city's "Take a Hike. Do it Right." checklist is a good habit for every outing.

Source: City of Phoenix, Piestewa Peak / Dreamy Draw.

When are the trailheads open, and what changes in summer?

Trailhead parking at Piestewa Peak and Dreamy Draw opens at 5 a.m. and closes at 7 p.m., while the trails themselves are open from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. From June 1 through September 30, the Piestewa Peak Trailhead parking lot stays open until 9 p.m. for cooler evening hikes.

On days when the National Weather Service issues an Extreme Heat Warning, the city restricts the Piestewa Peak Summit Trail and its associated trails from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. That makes fall through spring the easiest season on the trails, and it moves most summer hiking to sunrise.

Sources: City of Phoenix, Piestewa Peak / Dreamy Draw; City of Phoenix, Trail Heat Safety Program.

What should dog owners know before hiking the preserve?

Dogs can hike City of Phoenix trails on a leash six feet or shorter, but they are not allowed on trails when the temperature is above 100 degrees. Dogs are prohibited year-round on the Piestewa Peak Summit Trail. Owners must keep dogs on designated trails and pick up and carry out waste.

The city also recommends testing the ground temperature with your hand, bringing water for your dog, and watching for signs of heatstroke. For dog owners shopping for a home, a trailhead within walking distance can make the daily routine much easier during the cooler months.

Source: City of Phoenix, Trails.

What is changing at North Mountain Park?

The City of Phoenix plans to close North Mountain Park for renovations from December 2026 through early 2028. Other Phoenix Mountains Preserve trailheads stay open during the project, including the North Mountain Visitor Center, so 85028 residents who usually hike North Mountain can shift to the 40th Street, 32nd Street, or Dreamy Draw trailheads.

Closures and trail notices change through the year, so it is worth checking the city's trails page before you go.

Source: City of Phoenix, Trails.

How old are the Phoenix Mountains, and what are they made of?

According to the City of Phoenix, the mountains around Piestewa Peak formed about 14 million years ago, and much of the rock is schist. Mercury mines once operated in the Dreamy Draw area, and the land was used for mining and grazing before the city annexed it in 1959.

Look closely at the layered, flaky rock along the trail edges on your next hike; that is the schist, and it tells part of the story of how this land went from mining claims to a protected desert park in the middle of the city.

Source: City of Phoenix, Piestewa Peak / Dreamy Draw.

How should trail access factor into choosing a home in 85028?

Start by deciding how you will actually use the preserve. Daily hikers and dog owners often value a home within walking distance of a trailhead, while others prefer a few streets back with less weekend parking. Visiting a trailhead on a Saturday morning and again on a weekday evening shows the difference quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Which Phoenix Mountain Preserve trailhead is in the 85028 ZIP code?

The 40th Street Trailhead, at 9200 N. 40th Street, is in 85028. It serves the L.V. Yates Trail (#8), a moderate 2.5-mile out-and-back with about 220 feet of elevation gain that connects to the Charles M. Christiansen Memorial Trail (#100).

What are the hours for Phoenix Mountain Preserve trails near Piestewa Peak?

Trails are open from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m., and trailhead parking is open from 5 a.m. to 7 p.m. From June 1 through September 30, the Piestewa Peak Trailhead parking lot stays open until 9 p.m.

Do Phoenix trails close during extreme heat?

Some do. When the National Weather Service issues an Extreme Heat Warning, the City of Phoenix restricts the Piestewa Peak Summit Trail and its associated trails from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., along with select trails at Camelback Mountain and South Mountain.

Can I bring my dog on Phoenix Mountain Preserve trails?

Yes, on a leash six feet or shorter, with waste carried out. Dogs are not allowed on City of Phoenix trails when the temperature is above 100 degrees, and they are prohibited year-round on the Piestewa Peak Summit Trail.

Is North Mountain Park open?

The City of Phoenix plans to close North Mountain Park for renovations from December 2026 through early 2028. The North Mountain Visitor Center and other Phoenix Mountains Preserve trailheads remain open during the closure.
