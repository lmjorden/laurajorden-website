Relocating from California to Scottsdale: The 2027 Luxury Buyer's Playbook

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published September 26, 2026

Why are so many California homeowners moving to Scottsdale right now?

California remains the single largest source of Scottsdale's luxury buyers, and the California-to-Scottsdale pipeline is one of the most consistent trends in luxury real estate today. The draw is straightforward: buyers get resort-grade community amenities, top-tier schools, and meaningfully more house for their equity, without giving up the climate, golf, and outdoor lifestyle they're used to. Neighborhoods like DC Ranch, Silverleaf, Troon, Desert Mountain, Whisper Rock, and Estancia have become the default landing zones for this wave of relocating buyers.

What's the real tax math behind a California-to-Arizona move?

Arizona taxes income at a flat 2.5% rate, compared to California's top marginal rate of 13.3%. For a household earning $500,000 a year, that gap alone can mean roughly $50,000 or more in annual state income tax savings after the move — before accounting for the lower cost of housing. Arizona's property tax structure is favorable too: owner-occupied primary residences are taxed at an average effective rate of about 0.85% of assessed value, with annual increases in assessed value capped at 5%, so tax bills don't spike the way they can in some coastal markets.

How does today's Scottsdale luxury market compare to what Californians are used to?

Scottsdale's luxury segment is sitting in relatively balanced territory, with roughly four to five months of inventory at the top end of the market. That gives relocating buyers room to negotiate, request inspections, and make a considered decision, rather than facing the frenzied, over-asking bidding wars common in many California markets. It also means California equity tends to go further here: median prices in DC Ranch run $2 million and up, while Silverleaf sits at $5 million and up — numbers that often allow Californians to upsize meaningfully while lowering their total housing carry cost.

What should out-of-state buyers know about purchasing on a compressed timeline?

Many of my California clients start with video walkthroughs and narrow their search remotely before ever stepping onto the property in person, which works well as long as you have a local agent who can vet HOA documents, water rights, solar leases, and anything else that doesn't show up on a video call. Arizona is also an escrow state rather than an attorney-closing state, which tends to streamline the closing process compared to what buyers coming from some other states may expect. If your California home hasn't sold yet, we can also talk through bridge financing or contingent-offer strategies so the timing works on both ends.

Which Scottsdale neighborhoods tend to be the best fit for California transplants?

For buyers who want guard-gated privacy, golf, and resort-caliber amenities, DC Ranch, Silverleaf, Troon, Desert Mountain, Whisper Rock, and Estancia are the communities I most often point California clients toward. For buyers who want more walkability and a downtown feel closer to what they had in coastal California cities, Old Town Scottsdale and Arcadia tend to be a better match. The right answer really depends on whether you're moving for space and privacy or for a more urban, walk-to-dinner lifestyle — and that's usually the first conversation we have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

How much can I really save moving from California to Arizona?

It depends on income and home value, but the state income tax gap alone (2.5% flat in Arizona versus up to 13.3% in California) can mean tens of thousands of dollars a year for higher-earning households, on top of typically lower property taxes and cost of living.

Do I need to sell my California home before buying in Scottsdale?

Not necessarily. Many relocating buyers use bridge financing or a contingent offer strategy so they can secure a Scottsdale home before their California sale closes. I can walk you through the options that fit your specific timeline.

Is Scottsdale's luxury market as competitive as California's?

It's generally more balanced. With roughly four to five months of luxury inventory, Scottsdale buyers typically have more room to negotiate and complete due diligence than in many high-demand California markets.

What Scottsdale neighborhoods do most California buyers choose?

DC Ranch, Silverleaf, Troon, Desert Mountain, Whisper Rock, and Estancia are the most common landing spots for relocating California buyers seeking guard-gated privacy and golf-community amenities.

Can I buy a home in Scottsdale without visiting in person?

Yes. Video tours combined with a trusted local agent who can flag anything a camera won't show you make remote purchases common, especially for relocating executives on tight timelines.
