Relocating to Arizona: What to Know Before You Move to the Phoenix Area

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published October 27, 2026

What should you know before relocating to Arizona?

Relocating to Arizona usually means choosing a neighborhood in the Phoenix metro, known locally as the Valley, before choosing a house. Commute, lot size, and lifestyle change sharply from one area to the next. Plan around Arizona-specific details too: a flat state income tax, a primary-residence property classification, and new-resident steps at the MVD.

I have lived in the Valley for more than 35 years and have been licensed in Arizona since 2005, and the most common surprise I hear from people moving here is how different each part of the metro feels. A home in Paradise Valley, a condo near the Biltmore, and a golf property in North Scottsdale can sit a short drive apart and offer completely different daily lives. Getting clear on how you want to live narrows the search faster than any price filter.

Which part of the Valley fits your lifestyle?

It depends on how you want to spend your days. Paradise Valley offers acre-plus lots and privacy. Scottsdale ranges from walkable Old Town to North Scottsdale golf communities. Arcadia and the Biltmore put you close to central Phoenix. The Shea-Tatum corridor sits beside the Phoenix Mountain Preserve, and Fountain Hills trades city bustle for quiet views.

Here is how I describe the main areas to clients moving from out of state:

  • Paradise Valley: a residential town with large lots, mountain views, and resort dining close by.
  • Old Town Scottsdale, McCormick Ranch & Gainey Ranch, and North Scottsdale: everything from walkable restaurants and galleries to master-planned communities and golf.
  • Arcadia and the Biltmore: established central neighborhoods with citrus-lined streets, Camelback Mountain views, and a short drive to Sky Harbor and downtown Phoenix.
  • The Shea-Tatum corridor (85028): homes backing the trails of the Phoenix Mountain Preserve, right next to Paradise Valley.
  • Fountain Hills: a quieter town on the east side of the McDowell Mountains with expansive desert views.
  • The East Valley (Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert): newer communities and more space, often closer to East Valley employers.

How do Arizona taxes affect your move?

Arizona adopted a flat 2.5 percent individual income tax in 2023, replacing its graduated brackets, according to Northern Arizona University. For property taxes, the home you live in as your one primary residence is classified differently from a second home or rental, and only primary residences receive the state's Homeowner Rebate credit.

That classification matters if you are keeping a home in another state or buying here as a second home first. The Maricopa County Assessor explains that a primary residence is Legal Class 3, while a vacation home or rental is Class 4 and cannot receive the credit; an Arizona driver license or voter registration at the address is accepted as proof of residency. A tax advisor can tell you how a change of residency affects your own situation.

What do new residents need to do after moving to Arizona?

Once you become an Arizona resident, the state's MVD says you must register vehicles brought from another state as soon as you establish residency. Bring each vehicle's current title and out-of-state registration. Some vehicles in the Phoenix metro also need an emissions test first. You will also visit the MVD for an Arizona driver license.

According to ADOT's New to Arizona page, you are considered a resident if, for example, you stay in Arizona seven months or more in a calendar year or work here. Beyond the MVD, I give relocating clients a short checklist: confirm which electric utility serves the address, review HOA rules before you write an offer if the home is in a community, and line up homeowner's insurance early, since your current carrier may not write policies in Arizona.

When is the best time to move to Arizona and tour homes?

Fall through spring is the most comfortable time to tour homes and unpack, because the summer heat makes midday moves hard. If you buy in summer, schedule showings and moving trucks for early morning. One adjustment surprises newcomers: Arizona does not change its clocks for daylight saving time, so the time difference with home shifts twice a year.

As ABC15 Arizona has reported, Arizona has kept standard time year-round for decades, largely so evenings cool down sooner; the Navajo Nation is the exception. For clients coordinating a move with family or offices back home, that shifting time difference is worth putting on the calendar. When the rest of the country falls back on November 1, 2026, Arizona clocks stay put.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Which Phoenix-area neighborhoods are most popular with people relocating to Arizona?

It depends on lifestyle. Buyers who want space and privacy often look at Paradise Valley, those who want walkability look at Old Town Scottsdale, Arcadia and the Biltmore suit buyers who want central Phoenix, the Shea-Tatum corridor (85028) offers Phoenix Mountain Preserve access, and Fountain Hills and the East Valley offer quieter settings and more space.

What is Arizona's state income tax rate?

Arizona adopted a flat 2.5 percent individual income tax beginning in 2023, replacing its earlier graduated brackets, according to Northern Arizona University. Confirm how a change of residency affects your own return with a tax advisor.

How soon do I need to register my car after moving to Arizona?

According to the Arizona Department of Transportation's MVD, vehicles brought from another state must be registered as soon as you become an Arizona resident. Some vehicles in the Phoenix metro need an emissions test before registration.

Does Arizona observe daylight saving time?

No. Most of Arizona stays on standard time all year, so the time difference with other states changes when they switch clocks. The Navajo Nation is the exception and does observe daylight saving time.
