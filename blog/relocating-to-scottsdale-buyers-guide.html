Relocating to Scottsdale: A Guide for Out-of-State and International Buyers

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published August 1, 2026

Why are so many out-of-state and international buyers relocating to Scottsdale?

Scottsdale draws relocating buyers for a specific combination of reasons: no state income tax on Social Security, a favorable overall tax climate compared to states like California and Illinois, over 300 days of sunshine a year, and an airport — Phoenix Sky Harbor — with direct flights to most major U.S. cities and a growing list of international routes. Add in a genuinely walkable downtown, world-class golf, and a healthcare system anchored by the Mayo Clinic's Scottsdale campus, and it's easy to see why buyers from California, the Midwest, Canada, and increasingly Europe and Asia treat Scottsdale as a serious relocation destination rather than just a winter escape.

What's changed in the last several years is the number of buyers moving here full-time rather than seasonally. Remote and hybrid work has made it possible for executives and business owners to keep their careers based elsewhere while living in Scottsdale year-round.

What should out-of-state buyers know about buying before visiting in person?

It's entirely possible to purchase a home in Scottsdale without setting foot in Arizona until closing, and I represent out-of-state buyers this way regularly. The key is building a trustworthy set of eyes on the ground: a private walkthrough on video call for every serious property, a local inspector whose reports I review with you line by line, and comparable-sales data pulled in real time so you're never guessing at value.

Financing is usually the longer pole in the tent for out-of-state buyers, particularly if you're working with a lender who isn't licensed in Arizona. I typically recommend getting pre-approved with a lender experienced in Arizona transactions before you start seriously looking, so financing timelines don't become the bottleneck once you find the right property.

How does the process differ for international buyers?

International buyers can purchase real estate in Arizona with no citizenship or residency requirement — there's no visa needed simply to own property here. What does change is financing: most international buyers either purchase in cash or work with a small number of lenders who specialize in foreign national loans, which typically require larger down payments (often 30–40%) and additional documentation of income and assets held outside the U.S.

I also coordinate with tax and legal advisors who specialize in cross-border real estate, since how you take title (individually, through an LLC, through a trust) has real implications for U.S. tax reporting and estate planning that are worth getting right from the start.

Which Scottsdale-area neighborhoods suit relocating buyers best?

For buyers who want walkability, restaurants, and an active social calendar from day one, Old Town Scottsdale is usually the right fit. Buyers relocating with school-age children often land in McCormick Ranch, Gainey Ranch, or North Scottsdale for the combination of family-oriented amenities and top-rated schools. Buyers prioritizing privacy and space tend toward Paradise Valley or the Cactus Corridor, while golf-focused relocations gravitate toward North Scottsdale's gated communities like Silverleaf, Desert Mountain, and Troon.

What logistics should relocating buyers plan for beyond the purchase?

Beyond the transaction itself, plan for Arizona's vehicle registration and driver's license timelines if you're becoming a resident, research whether your current homeowner's insurance carrier operates in Arizona (many out-of-state buyers switch), and budget time to research HOA rules if you're buying in a community — they vary significantly in scope from one Scottsdale neighborhood to the next. I typically walk relocating clients through this full checklist well before closing so nothing catches you off guard in the first few weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Can I buy a home in Scottsdale without visiting in person first?

Yes. Many out-of-state and international buyers purchase in Scottsdale sight-unseen using video walkthroughs, third-party inspections, and detailed comparable-sales data, visiting only for closing or move-in.

Do international buyers face different requirements when purchasing U.S. real estate?

There is no citizenship or residency requirement to own property in Arizona. The main difference is financing, since most international buyers purchase in cash or use lenders who specialize in foreign national loans with larger down payment requirements.

What visa or residency status is needed to buy property in Arizona?

None. Foreign nationals can purchase and hold real estate in Arizona regardless of visa or residency status, though how you take title has tax and estate planning implications worth discussing with an advisor.

Which Scottsdale neighborhoods are best for buyers relocating from out of state?

It depends on priorities: Old Town Scottsdale for walkability, McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch for families and schools, Paradise Valley and Cactus Corridor for privacy, and North Scottsdale communities like Silverleaf and Desert Mountain for golf.
