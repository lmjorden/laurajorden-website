Scottsdale Luxury Real Estate Market Forecast: 2027 Outlook

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published August 15, 2026

Where does the Scottsdale luxury market stand heading into 2027?

Scottsdale enters 2027 in a more balanced position than it held for most of the previous several years. Citywide median sale prices have settled into the high $800,000s to $968,000 range, inventory has grown, and days on market have lengthened across most price tiers — a meaningful shift from the tightly-supplied market of a few years ago. The exception is the $2M+ luxury segment, which has continued to outperform, with sales up more than 25% year-over-year even as the broader market cooled.

Is Scottsdale still a buyer's market or a seller's market?

It depends heavily on price point and neighborhood. In the sub-$1.5M range across most of Scottsdale, conditions now favor buyers, with more inventory to choose from and more room to negotiate on price and terms. Above $2M, and especially in premium micro-markets like Paradise Valley's Mummy Mountain corridor and guard-gated communities like Silverleaf, demand has remained strong enough that well-priced, well-presented homes are still moving quickly and sellers retain real leverage.

What's driving the $2M+ segment's outperformance?

Several factors are converging: continued relocation demand from higher-tax states, the rise of remote and hybrid work keeping high earners in Scottsdale year-round rather than just seasonally, and a genuine scarcity of new inventory in the most desirable guard-gated and estate-lot communities, where land constraints limit new construction. Buyers at this level are also less financing-dependent, which insulates the segment somewhat from interest rate sensitivity that affects the broader market more directly.

Which submarkets are positioned to see the strongest 2027 activity?

North Scottsdale's established golf communities, the Cactus Corridor, and Paradise Valley are positioned to remain the most active luxury submarkets heading into 2027, supported by limited new supply and consistent relocation and second-home demand. Old Town Scottsdale's luxury condo and townhome market is also worth watching, as buyers who want lock-and-leave luxury without estate-level maintenance continue to compete for a genuinely limited number of new-construction and recently built units.

What should buyers and sellers do differently heading into 2027?

Buyers working in the sub-$1.5M range have more negotiating room than they've had in years and should use it — on price, closing cost credits, and timeline flexibility. Buyers competing for premium inventory above $2M should expect to move decisively when the right property appears, since that segment hasn't slowed the way the broader market has. Sellers at any price point benefit from sharper pricing and stronger presentation than was necessary a few years ago; homes that are priced accurately and shown well are still selling at a healthy pace, while overpriced listings are sitting longer than sellers may expect.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Is Scottsdale real estate expected to slow down in 2027?

The broader market has already moderated, with more inventory and longer days on market than in previous years. The $2M+ luxury segment, however, has continued to outperform and shows no signs of slowing heading into 2027.

Is now a good time to buy in Scottsdale?

For buyers under roughly $1.5 million, current conditions offer more inventory and negotiating room than the market has seen in years. Above $2 million, well-priced homes in desirable communities are still moving quickly, so buyers at that level should be prepared to act decisively.

Why is the $2M+ Scottsdale market outperforming the rest?

Continued relocation from higher-tax states, remote work keeping high earners in Scottsdale year-round, and limited new inventory in premium guard-gated and estate-lot communities are all supporting demand at the top of the market.

Which Scottsdale neighborhoods are expected to be strongest in 2027?

North Scottsdale's established golf communities, the Cactus Corridor, and Paradise Valley are positioned for the strongest 2027 activity, along with Old Town Scottsdale's limited luxury condo and townhome supply.

Should sellers price differently in today's market than a few years ago?

Yes. With more inventory and longer days on market across most price tiers, accurate pricing and strong presentation matter more than they did during the tightest years of the market, when overpricing carried less risk.
