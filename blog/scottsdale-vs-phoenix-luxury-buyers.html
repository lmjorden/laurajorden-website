Scottsdale vs. Phoenix: Where Luxury Buyers Should Really Be Looking in 2027

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published September 19, 2026

How do Scottsdale and Phoenix compare for luxury buyers?

Scottsdale and Phoenix share an airport, a climate, and increasingly a labor market, but they offer meaningfully different luxury real estate experiences. Scottsdale's luxury market is built around golf communities, resort culture, and a walkable downtown core, with a citywide median sale price in the high $800,000s to $968,000 range and premium neighborhoods running well above that. Phoenix, by contrast, offers a broader mix of historic luxury neighborhoods, downtown high-rise living, and mountain-view estate lots, generally at a lower price per square foot than comparable Scottsdale product.

Where in Phoenix competes with Scottsdale at the luxury level?

The Biltmore area — which straddles the Phoenix-Scottsdale line and is anchored by the historic Arizona Biltmore resort and its golf course — is Phoenix's closest direct answer to Scottsdale's resort-luxury lifestyle. The Downtown Phoenix Historic District, with its Spanish Colonial and craftsman-era homes, appeals to buyers who want architectural character and walkability closer to Phoenix's growing downtown core. Encanto-Palmcroft, one of Phoenix's original prestige neighborhoods, offers mature, palm-lined streets and historic estate homes at prices that often undercut comparable Scottsdale inventory.

What are the practical differences buyers notice day to day?

Scottsdale tends to feel more resort-oriented day to day — more golf, more spa culture, more of a built-in luxury social calendar. Phoenix feels more urban and varied, with a genuinely walkable, rapidly developing downtown, a stronger arts and culture scene, and generally shorter commutes to the city's largest employers. Buyers who prioritize a consistent, curated luxury lifestyle tend to prefer Scottsdale; buyers who want more architectural and neighborhood diversity, along with big-city energy, often prefer Phoenix.

Does one city offer better value right now?

On a pure price-per-square-foot basis, Phoenix's luxury neighborhoods generally offer better value than comparable Scottsdale product, particularly in historic districts where character and lot size can exceed what the same budget buys in Scottsdale. That said, Scottsdale's premium submarkets — particularly the $2M+ segment, where sales were up more than 25% year-over-year as of early 2026 — have shown stronger demand resilience, which matters for buyers thinking about long-term appreciation as much as immediate value.

How should buyers decide between the two?

I typically encourage buyers to start with lifestyle rather than price: if resort amenities, golf, and a curated luxury environment matter most, Scottsdale is usually the right answer even at a premium. If architectural character, urban walkability, and value per square foot matter more, Phoenix's luxury neighborhoods deserve a serious look. Many of my clients end up touring both before deciding, and that comparison alone is often the fastest way to clarify what actually matters to you.

Common Questions

Is Phoenix cheaper than Scottsdale for luxury homes?

Generally yes, on a price-per-square-foot basis. Phoenix's luxury neighborhoods, including the Biltmore area and Encanto-Palmcroft, often offer more space and historic character than a comparable budget buys in Scottsdale.

What Phoenix neighborhood is most comparable to Scottsdale?

The Biltmore area, which straddles the Phoenix-Scottsdale line and is anchored by the historic Arizona Biltmore resort and golf course, is Phoenix's closest equivalent to Scottsdale's resort-luxury lifestyle.

Which is better for luxury living, Scottsdale or Phoenix?

It depends on lifestyle priorities. Scottsdale offers a more resort-oriented, golf-centered luxury lifestyle, while Phoenix offers more architectural variety, urban walkability, and generally better value per square foot.

Is Scottsdale's luxury market outperforming Phoenix right now?

Scottsdale's $2M+ segment has shown particularly strong demand, with sales up more than 25% year-over-year as of early 2026, which is a point in Scottsdale's favor for buyers focused on long-term appreciation.
