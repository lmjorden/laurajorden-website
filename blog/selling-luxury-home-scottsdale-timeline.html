Selling Your Scottsdale Home for Top Dollar: A Luxury Seller's Timeline

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published August 29, 2026

How far in advance should luxury sellers start preparing?

For a luxury home, I recommend starting the conversation 60 to 90 days before you want to list. That window gives enough time for a pre-listing walkthrough to identify any repairs or updates worth making, for professional photography and, where appropriate, video or drone footage to be scheduled and shot in good light, and for pricing strategy to be built around genuinely current comparable sales rather than data that's already a few months stale in a market that's been shifting.

What does the pre-listing preparation phase actually involve?

Beyond standard staging, luxury pre-listing prep typically includes a pre-inspection so you can address issues before a buyer's inspector finds them, professional deep cleaning and landscaping refresh, and a frank conversation about which updates will actually move the needle on price versus which are better left for the next owner to customize. In today's market, with more inventory for buyers to compare against, presentation matters more than it did a few years ago — homes that show well are the ones still selling quickly.

What does the timeline look like from listing to close?

Once listed, a well-priced luxury home in a desirable Scottsdale submarket is typically under contract within 30 to 60 days, though that window has lengthened somewhat as overall days on market have increased across the broader market. From an accepted offer, plan on another 30 to 45 days to close, covering inspection, appraisal, and loan underwriting if the buyer is financing. All told, sellers should plan for a 90 to 150 day process from initial preparation to closing, longer for ultra-luxury or highly specialized properties with a smaller pool of qualified buyers.

What's different about selling in today's more balanced market?

Inventory has grown and days on market have lengthened across most of Scottsdale, which means buyers have more to compare your home against than they did a few years ago. The exception is the $2M+ segment, where demand has stayed strong and sales were up more than 25% year-over-year as of early 2026 — well-priced luxury homes are still moving at a healthy pace, but overpriced ones now sit long enough that sellers notice.

What mistakes cost luxury sellers the most money?

The single most expensive mistake is overpricing at launch. Buyers and their agents track a listing's days on market closely, and a price reduction after 60-90 days on the market almost always nets less than pricing accurately from day one would have. The second most common mistake is skipping professional photography and video for a property in this price range — in a market where buyers are scrolling through dozens of comparable listings, the first impression online determines whether they ever request a showing at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

How long does it take to sell a luxury home in Scottsdale?

A well-priced luxury home in a desirable Scottsdale submarket typically goes under contract within 30 to 60 days, with another 30 to 45 days to close, for a total process of roughly 90 to 150 days including preparation.

How far in advance should I start preparing to sell a luxury home?

Sixty to 90 days before listing is ideal, giving time for a pre-listing walkthrough, any needed repairs or updates, and professional photography scheduled in good light.

Is it still a seller's market for luxury homes in Scottsdale?

It depends on price point. The $2M+ segment has continued to outperform with strong buyer demand, while the broader market has become more balanced with increased inventory and longer days on market.

What's the biggest pricing mistake luxury sellers make?

Overpricing at launch. Homes that sit on the market and require a price reduction after 60 to 90 days typically net less than if they had been priced accurately from the start.

Is professional photography necessary for a luxury listing?

Yes. With buyers comparing dozens of listings online before requesting a showing, professional photography and video are often what determines whether a buyer engages with a listing at all.
