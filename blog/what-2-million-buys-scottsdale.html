What $2M+ Buys You in Scottsdale Right Now: A Neighborhood Price Guide

By Laura Jorden, Compass  ·  Published August 8, 2026

What does $2 million actually buy in today's Scottsdale market?

Two million dollars is a meaningful entry point in Scottsdale, but what it buys varies enormously by neighborhood. Recent market data puts Scottsdale's citywide median sale price in the high $800,000s to $968,000 range, with the broader luxury segment (homes over $1M) carrying a median closer to $1.85M — which means $2M sits solidly inside the luxury tier rather than at its ceiling. In North Scottsdale's golf and gated communities, where the median runs closer to $1.3M, $2M typically buys a well-appointed single-family home with a pool and golf-course or mountain views. In Old Town Scottsdale, where the luxury entry point is closer to $1.7M, $2M buys either a premium single-family home or a high-end condo or townhome in one of the walkable core neighborhoods.

How do prices compare across Scottsdale's luxury neighborhoods?

In McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch, $2M generally buys a spacious lakefront or golf-adjacent home with significant lot size and mature landscaping. In the Cactus Corridor, known for its larger estate lots, $2M is often an entry-level price for a custom home rather than a top-of-market one. Paradise Valley is a different conversation entirely: with an overall median that has run well above $3M and the Mummy Mountain corridor (85253) often listing between $6M and $15M, $2M in Paradise Valley typically buys one of the market's older or smaller homes on a standard lot rather than a true estate property.

What's happening in the $2M+ segment right now?

The $2M+ price tier has been outperforming lower price bands, with sales in this segment up more than 25% year-over-year as of early 2026 — a sign that high-net-worth demand for Scottsdale has stayed strong even as the broader market has become more balanced. At the same time, overall inventory has increased and days on market have lengthened compared to the previous few years, which gives buyers at this price point real room to negotiate on price, closing costs, and timeline in a way that wasn't true during the tightest years of the market.

Where does $2M+ go furthest, and where does it go the least far?

Dollar for dollar, $2M currently stretches furthest in North Scottsdale's established (non-guard-gated) communities and in parts of the Shea and Cactus Corridors, where you can find larger lots and more recently updated homes than the same budget would buy in Old Town or Paradise Valley. It goes the least far in Paradise Valley's premium micro-markets and in guard-gated communities like Silverleaf, where median list prices have run in the $6M+ range and $2M is closer to a starting point for smaller homes than a comfortable mid-market budget.

What should buyers expect to compromise on at this price point?

At $2M, most buyers end up choosing between lot size, location, and finish level rather than getting all three at the top of their range. A buyer prioritizing a walkable Old Town location may accept a smaller lot or a condo instead of a single-family home; a buyer prioritizing acreage in the Cactus Corridor may need to plan for updates rather than buying fully renovated. Knowing which trade-off matters most to you before you start touring homes makes the search significantly more efficient.
