Equestrian Properties in Scottsdale and the Phoenix Area

Where to find horse property around the Valley, how zoning works, and what to check before you buy. A guide from Laura Jorden, Compass.

Where are the horse properties in the Phoenix area?

Horse properties are spread across the Valley, and each area has its own feel and rules. These are the areas buyers ask about most:

  • North Scottsdale: acreage homes near Pinnacle Peak and the McDowell Sonoran Preserve, whose trail system is open to horseback riding
  • Rio Verde Foothills: larger, more rural parcels in unincorporated Maricopa County, east of Scottsdale
  • Cave Creek: a ranch-style town with an equestrian heritage and trail access north of Phoenix
  • East Valley: Queen Creek and parts of south Gilbert, with flatter acreage and newer custom homes

Scottsdale is also home to WestWorld of Scottsdale, which hosts major equestrian events including the annual Scottsdale Arabian Horse Show.

How does zoning work for horses?

Rules vary by city, town, and county, and they can vary again by subdivision. The City of Scottsdale says it does not regulate the number of horses kept for personal use on a single-family lot with a city-approved home, but HOA rules, CC&Rs, deed restrictions, and Maricopa County health rules can still apply. Commercial boarding or training is regulated separately.

Neighboring towns and unincorporated county land each set their own limits, so the same acreage can carry very different rights a few miles apart. Always confirm horse privileges in writing with the governing city, town, or county before you buy.

What should buyers check before buying a horse property?

  • Zoning and restrictions: confirm horse privileges with the city, town, or county, and read the CC&Rs and any deed restrictions.
  • Water: find out whether the home uses city water, a private or shared well, or hauled water. Water is a bigger question in some rural areas than buyers expect.
  • Well and septic: inspect both, and make sure the leach field isn't under a paddock.
  • Drainage and washes: check where water runs during monsoon storms and whether any part of the property sits in a flood zone.
  • Barns, arenas, and shade: confirm existing structures were permitted, and plan shade and shelter for summer heat.
  • Trail access: see whether you can ride out from the property or will need to trailer to trailheads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions

Where can you buy horse property near Scottsdale?

Popular areas for horse property include parts of North Scottsdale near Pinnacle Peak and the McDowell Sonoran Preserve, the Rio Verde Foothills, Cave Creek, and the East Valley, particularly Queen Creek and parts of south Gilbert. The right fit depends on acreage, trail access, and how many horses you plan to keep.

How many horses can I keep on a Scottsdale property?

According to the City of Scottsdale, the city does not regulate the number of horses kept for the homeowner's personal use on a single-family lot with a city-approved home. HOA rules, CC&Rs, deed restrictions, and Maricopa County health rules can still limit animals, so check each property individually.

Can I board other people's horses on my property?

Commercial boarding or training is regulated separately from personal use, and in Scottsdale it generally requires city approval. Check with the city or town before buying if boarding income is part of your plan.

What should I inspect on a horse property?

Beyond a standard home inspection, check the well and septic systems, the water source, drainage and washes, fencing, barn and arena permits, and any recorded restrictions on animals.
